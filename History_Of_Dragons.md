# The History of the Dragons of Aerthys

## Prologue: Before the Fire

Before the first man ever looked up at the sky and gave a name to fear, dragons already soared through the air of Aerthys. They were not born of chaos, as the old legends claimed, nor were they created by capricious gods. They were born of stone itself, from mountains that breathed slowly for millennia until their mineral hearts became something alive.

The first dragons had no names, because there was no one to name them. They lived in the Ember Peaks, a belt of volcanoes that split the continent in two, and there they slept for centuries, feeding on the heat of the earth. By the time they finally woke, the world already had forests, oceans, and the first creatures crawling through the mud.

## I. The Age of the Five Lineages

Over time, the dragons split into five lineages, each bound to an element and a region of the world.

**The Ignisaurians**, scaled in red and gold, dwelt in the Ember Peaks and were the most numerous of all. Their breath could melt iron, and their temperament was as volatile as the volcanoes that birthed them.

**The Glacyrids**, a nearly translucent pale blue, lived in the Silent Peaks to the north, where the ice never melted. They were the most patient of all the lineages, capable of remaining motionless for years, simply watching.

**The Tempests**, gray as storm clouds, nested on the cliffs of the Shattered Coast. They flew faster than any other living creature, and their roar alone could summon real storms.

**The Verdants**, green as deep moss, hid within the Everwood, a forest so vast no human had ever crossed it in full. They were healers and guardians, not warriors.

**The Umbralts**, black as a moonless night, lived underground, in the Deep Caverns. Little was known of them, and what little was known frightened the other four lineages nearly as much as it frightened humans.

For thousands of years, these five lineages coexisted with barely any contact, each ruling its territory under a kind of unspoken treaty that no one had ever signed but that everyone respected.

## II. The Coming of Man

When the first humans crossed the mountains and settled in the fertile valleys at the heart of Aerthys, the dragons watched them with curiosity more than hostility. They were fragile creatures, short-lived, who built strange things out of wood and stone.

For generations, humans and dragons lived at a respectful distance. The oldest surviving accounts — carved into clay tablets found among the ruins of Doreth — speak of shepherds who saw winged shadows cross the sky at dusk, but rarely of direct encounters.

Everything changed with Kaelen Brandtower.

Kaelen was a blacksmith from a village called Ostbrun, dangerously close to the Ember Peaks. One especially harsh winter night, a young Ignisaurian, wounded after a territorial fight with another of its own kind, fell from the sky near his forge. Instead of fleeing or reaching for a weapon, Kaelen did something no one had ever done before: he offered help.

He treated the dragon's wounds with the same salves he used for burns from his own trade, and over the weeks it took the creature to regain the strength to fly, Kaelen spoke to it. He expected no answer. But the dragon, who called himself Vorenth in the old tongue of his lineage, could understand him — and in time, learned to answer back.

It was the first documented bond between a human and a dragon. It would not be the last.

## III. The Pact of Ostbrun

Word that a blacksmith spoke with dragons spread like fire itself. Some came to kill Vorenth, believing him a threat; others came to study the phenomenon; a few came simply to look.

Vorenth, for his part, told the other Ignisaurians what had happened, and they, intrigued, began cautiously approaching the human settlements near the mountains. Not every encounter ended well. Villages were burned by frightened or furious dragons, and dragons were hunted by men who saw fortune in their scales and bones.

But in Ostbrun, something different was taking shape. Kaelen, now elderly, convinced the village elders and the young dragons who frequented the area to strike an agreement: the dragons would protect the village from other threats — bandits, creatures from the Everwood, harsh winters — in exchange for humans never hunting or trading dragon parts, nor ever attempting to capture them.

This agreement became known as the Pact of Ostbrun, and although it took generations to spread beyond that small village, it laid the foundation for what would later be called the Age of Coexistence.

## IV. The War of the Broken Scales

Not everything was peaceful, however. Two hundred years after Kaelen's death, an ambitious kingdom called Cadrenor, ruled by King Malwyn the Greedy, decided that dragons were not allies but resources. Malwyn ordered the construction of giant traps in the Ember Peaks, disguised as sanctuaries of peace, meant to capture Ignisaurians and force them into service as weapons of war.

When Vorenth — now old, but still the most respected dragon of his lineage — discovered the deception, he summoned all five lineages for the first time in recorded history. It was an unprecedented act: Ignisaurians, Glacyrids, Tempests, Verdants, and even the elusive Umbralts gathered in a council that lasted seven days and seven nights atop the oldest volcano, Mount Ashen.

From that council came the decision to act together. What followed is known in the history books as the War of the Broken Scales, a brief but devastating conflict. The dragons did not attack cities or civilians; instead, they systematically destroyed every trap, every fortress dedicated to dragon-hunting, and finally surrounded Malwyn's castle in an aerial siege no human army could break.

Malwyn surrendered on the tenth day, not because his castle had fallen, but because the Tempests had unleashed unrelenting storms over Cadrenor for a week straight, ruining harvests and terrifying the population, who demanded peace at any cost.

The treaty that followed, signed among the ruins of one of the destroyed traps, established for the first time laws extending beyond a single village: no kingdom could hunt dragons, and in exchange, the dragons pledged not to interfere in human political affairs unless directly provoked.

## V. The Golden Age

The centuries that followed the War of the Broken Scales became known as the Golden Age. Entire academies devoted themselves to the study of the draconic tongue; the Verdants shared healing secrets that saved countless lives during the plagues that ravaged the south; the Glacyrids taught the peoples of the north to survive winters that had once decimated them.

It was also the age in which the first Dragon Riders emerged — humans who, after generations of mutual trust, were accepted by individual dragons as flight companions. The bond between a rider and their dragon was not one of dominance, but of mutual choice: a dragon might reject a hundred candidates and accept the hundred-and-first for reasons no human ever fully understood.

The most famous of these riders was Ysenna Skyborn, a woman born in a fishing village on the Shattered Coast, chosen by a Tempest named Ryvex when she was barely twelve years old. Together, Ysenna and Ryvex mapped regions of Aerthys no human had ever seen, and their travel chronicles — preserved to this day in the Great Library of Doreth — remain the most complete source that exists on the continent's geography of that era.

## VI. The Shadow of the Deep Caverns

But even in the Golden Age, not everything was light. The Umbralts, the most reclusive lineage, began showing signs of unease. Something was stirring in the depths of the Caverns, something even they did not fully understand: an ancient presence, older than the five lineages, that some called the Root Devourer.

It was not a dragon, nor any common beast. Umbralt accounts spoke of a creature made of void, slowly feeding on the very life of the caverns, expanding year after year. For generations, the Umbralts fought this threat alone, hiding it even from the other lineages out of pride, or fear of appearing weak.

When they finally asked for help — an act that cost them a chasm of dignity — it was already too late to contain the Devourer within the caverns. It emerged to the surface on what is now known as the Night of the Collapse, a night when the sky itself seemed to darken over the region of Kharzul.

## VII. The Last Battle of the Five Lineages

It was in that moment that the union forged during the War of the Broken Scales proved its true worth. The five lineages, together with a band of riders led by the descendants of Ysenna Skyborn, faced the Root Devourer in what is known as the Last Battle, though historians prefer to call it the Battle of Kharzul, to avoid the superstition that naming it "last" might invite a worse one still to come.

The battle lasted three days. The Ignisaurians attacked with fire that the Devourer simply absorbed; the Glacyrids tried to freeze it, without success; the Tempests lashed it with lightning that barely made it stagger. It was the Verdants who discovered the creature's weakness: it did not feed on matter, but on active life, on will. And the Umbralts, who understood its nature best, devised a desperate plan: surround it in a circle of absolute stillness, denying it any source of living energy to draw upon.

It was a sacrifice. Dozens of dragons from all five lineages formed the circle and remained utterly motionless, suppressing even their own heartbeats for what felt like an eternity, while the Devourer, starving and confused, began to collapse in on itself until it crumbled into dust carried off by the wind toward the sea.

Not every dragon in the circle survived. It is said that those who died that day became the first shooting stars the people of Kharzul see each autumn, and that if one of those trails of light crosses the sky while a dragon flies nearby, it is a sign that the fallen still watch over their kin.

## VIII. The Legacy

Today, centuries after the Battle of Kharzul, the dragons of Aerthys are no longer the dominant force they once were. Their numbers have dwindled, not from hunting — which remains forbidden by law and by custom across nearly the whole continent — but because dragons, unlike humans, do not multiply easily: a female of any lineage lays, at most, one egg every twenty years.

Even so, their presence still shapes life across Aerthys. In Ostbrun, now a thriving city, the memory of Kaelen Brandtower and Vorenth is still honored with an annual festival called the Night of the First Scales. In the Ember Peaks, the Ignisaurians continue to guard the mountain passes as they have since time immemorial. And on clear autumn nights, when shooting stars streak across the sky over Kharzul, elders still tell children the story of the dragons who held still to save the world.

Dragon riders, though rare, still exist, chosen not by lineage or power, but by something neither humans nor dragons have ever fully managed to explain: a spark of mutual recognition between two very different creatures who, against all logic, chose to trust one another.

And deep within the Caverns, the Umbralts still keep watch — in case something else ever wakes in the dark.
